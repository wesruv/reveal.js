Cursed
Feedback Loops

  • Sometimes we have cursed feedback loops, incenting behavior that produces worse results
  • Daniel Kahneman (Nobel Prize winning psychologist)
    was teaching Air Force flight instructors
    the psychology of effective training
  • Kahneman was giving a speech about
    an important principle of skill training:
    rewards for improved performance work better than punishment for mistakes.
  • After his 'enthusiastic speech', one of the
    seasoned pilot instructors stood up to say
    positive reinforcement wasn't optimal for cadets
  • In his long experience when
    he praised a cadet for a good maneuver,
    the next time they performed worse!
  • On the other hand,
    when he screams into their headset
    after a bad maneuver the often improved the next time.
  • The instructor was experience is accurate,
    but also completely wrong.

  • Responsible for intuition, our "gut"
  • Little or no voluntary control
  • Walking and talking

System 2

  • Effortful and much slower than System 1
  • Only runs when needed
  • Responsible for conscious choice and concentration
  • If we're walking and talking, 17 x 24
  • All day we have to make a myriad of little decisions

Cognitive Errors

  • Before I get too much into them, I've got bad news.
    • Knowing these about cognitive errors and how they happen doesn't mean you're less likely to have them.
    • These are bugs in the machine, we all experience them, all the time.

Substituting Questions

  • Our minds will subconsciously substitute easier questions instead of trying to figure out the actual (harder) question,
  • For example:
    • If asked to donate to a non-profit, the question you're probably answering is 'how many stories of this problem can I think of' and 'how strongly do I feel about it'
    • If asked how safe it is in a city or country, you're probably going to answer the question 'how many stories about crime or violence can I think of' and maybe questioning how much you've even heard about the place.

Encourage (or find) a Learning Culture

  • The best groups of professionals, teams and projects enjoyed figuring out issues and getting better, even if it meant they were mistaken.

There are two key aspects to a Learning Culture

  • Psychological Safety
  • Accountability

Psychological Safety

@todo
  • The belief that you won't be punished or humiliated for speaking up with ideas, questions, concerns, or mistakes.
  • It requires that we be able to challenge authority
  • Without this, people won't bring up problems, or avoid conflict; even when there's a **significant problem**.
  • In a lot of public discourse, this gets confused with not saying anything challenging
  • It should mean the opposite; it should make room for lively discussion and disagreements.
  • It means those conversations involve participants feeling heard and respected

Accountability

  • Without accountability, there is no reason to bring up issues.
  • Not every issue has to be solved, but change based on feedback should not be uncommon.
  • This isn't about blame, punishment, shame, or guilt;
    • but us against the problem, because bad outcomes and mistakes happen.
  • It's not that people need to be punished for mistakes, but that we need to see change based on things not going well.
